## The trajectory

The last three roles form a clean arc. Each step increased scope and decreased
organizational support. This is a trajectory, not a random sequence, and it is
the strongest structural argument for a Staff or Founding role.

| | Turnitin 2018-2022 | Slingshot 2022-2023 | Sysgit 2023-2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Design org | 20 designers, formal process and critique | Company design team, shared design system, group critique | None. Morgan is the design function |
| On the product | Solo | Solo | Solo |
| PM | Yes, a real one | Rotating cast, none lasted | None. Morgan is part-time PM |
| Manager | Yes | **Never had one.** Nominally VP Product | Reports to VP Engineering |
| Engineers | 8-9, front end and ML | ~7 | ~6, and Morgan pushes comparable code |
| People leadership | **Lead to 3 junior designers** | No | No |
| Critique | Formal | Group critique | None. Built his own crit skill |

## What this answers

**"Can you work inside a design org you do not own?"** Yes, for four years, in a
20-person org with formal process, contributing to a design system he did not
control.

**"Can you mentor?"** Yes. Lead to three junior designers at Turnitin. This is the
one thing a pure founding-designer profile usually cannot evidence, and it is
frequently a Staff-level requirement.

**"Three years without critique, can you take feedback?"** He had formal critique
at Turnitin and group critique at Slingshot. He lost it at Sysgit and built a
substitute rather than adapting to its absence.

**"Can you operate with nothing?"** Yes. Sysgit.

## How to use it

The range is the argument. Most candidates can evidence one column. Morgan can
evidence three, in sequence, with increasing scope. State it as a progression,
not as three unrelated jobs.
