# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

**Primary (mixed audience, hiring-weighted):**

- **Hiring managers and design leaders** at enterprise and hard-tech companies evaluating Morgan for senior product design roles. They arrive skeptical of portfolio theater — they want evidence of systems thinking, constraint navigation, and shipped outcomes in decision-heavy domains.
- **Prospective clients** seeking contract or embedded design work on complex B2B products, especially where field research and operational context matter.
- **Design peers** looking for credible case study depth — how decisions were made, not just what screens look like.

**Context when using:** Quick scan on mobile or between meetings; deeper read on case studies when a role or project warrants it. They are not browsing for inspiration — they are evaluating whether Morgan can operate in their environment.

## Product Purpose

A product design portfolio that demonstrates expertise in **decision-heavy enterprise software** — aerospace, healthcare, academic integrity, critical infrastructure — where design choices carry operational, financial, and regulatory weight.

Success looks like:
- A visitor understands Morgan's niche within 10 seconds (hard-tech, field-embedded, systems rigor).
- Case studies prove depth: problem framing, constraints, decisions, system explanation, outcomes — not just UI galleries.
- The portfolio itself practices what it preaches: precise craft, traceability, and room for human judgement.
- Contact is frictionless for the audiences that matter (email CTA, clear credibility signals).

## Brand Personality

**Three words:** Field-tested · Rigorous · Unmistakable

**Voice:** Confident without performance. Wyoming-raised, dirt-tested. Speaks to operators and experts, not to a generic "designer who likes coffee and tacos." Editorial when it serves clarity; instrument-panel precise for metadata and system labels.

**Emotional goals:**
- **Confidence** — this person has been in the room where decisions matter.
- **Credibility** — fieldwork credentials (ER trauma centers, Yellowstone, launchpads, underfunded schools) are proof, not decoration.

**References (named):**
- Editorial specimen pages that treat type as structure, not decoration.
- Field-research and operations culture — embedded with users, not observing from a studio.

## Anti-references

- **Generic designer portfolios** — coffee-and-tacos personality, Dribbble-shot polish without narrative, beige SaaS landing page aesthetics, "creative" headshots and hobby lists as substitute for work depth.
- **AI-generated landing page grammar** — cream/sand body backgrounds, small uppercase eyebrow kickers on every section, numbered section markers (01 / 02 / 03) as scaffolding, identical icon-heading-text card grids, gradient text, glassmorphism cards, hero-metric templates.
- **Loud accent misuse** — chartreuse (glow) beyond ~2.5% of any viewport; ember on buttons or primary CTAs.
- **Portfolio theater** — animations and visual effects that obscure rather than explain systems thinking.

## Design Principles

1. **Field-tested over portfolio theater.** Credibility comes from embedded research and operational context, not from visual performance. The proof strip and case study depth do the selling.

2. **Show the system, not just the screens.** Every case study must explain constraints, decisions, and outcomes — the thinking behind the interface, not a gallery of polished mocks.

3. **Rigor with room for human judgement.** Design for expert operators who need traceability and edge-state clarity, not simplified consumer flows.

4. **Practice what you preach.** The portfolio chrome must meet the same craft bar as the work it showcases — precise type, disciplined accent use, accessible contrast, intentional motion.

5. **Unmistakable, not loud.** Distinctiveness through the Greenhouse identity (drenched green, glow accent, jackalope, mono labels) — not through novelty effects or trend-chasing layout patterns.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Target **WCAG 2.1 AA** for text contrast on dark greenhouse surfaces (bone/sage on moss/fern, glow on moss for buttons).
- **Reduced motion:** respect `prefers-reduced-motion` for all transitions and hover animations; provide instant or crossfade alternatives.
- **Keyboard navigation:** all interactive elements (buttons, cards, nav links, before/after handles) must be focusable with visible focus states.
- **Color:** glow and ember accents must not be the sole carrier of meaning — pair with text labels, borders, or typographic hierarchy.
