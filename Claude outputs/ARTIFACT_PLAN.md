## 1. Three things your answers changed

### a. Sysgit should be photographed, not recreated

The old plan put interactive recreations at the top because I assumed you could
not show the product. You can. Sysgit is live and freely showable except the MCP
server.

That inverts the recommendation. A real capture of the shipped diagram editor is
**more** credible than a recreation and **cheaper** to produce. A recreation
invites the question "is this what actually shipped, or what you wish had
shipped." A screenshot does not.

Recreations still earn their place in exactly one case: when the decision you are
illustrating is about **behavior over time** and a still frame cannot carry it.
Two slots qualify. Everything else on Sysgit is a capture.

### b. The annotated CDM printout has to change or go

Current caption: *"Annotated CDM printout from an SME session."*

If you do not have those printouts, that slot cannot be filled. Producing an
image of expert annotations that no expert made is fabrication, and it is the
same category of error as the Command Center case study, not a lesser one. The
fact that the session really happened does not make a manufactured artifact of it
honest.

Three ways out, in order of preference:

1. **Check whether you actually have them.** Photos on an old phone, a scan in
   Drive, anything in the Figma files. A real one is worth more than the next two
   options combined.
2. **Replace the slot with a diagram of the method**, captioned as a diagram.
   "How we found out what mattered in a CDM: printouts, SMEs, circles." It shows
   the method without pretending to be the evidence.
3. **Cut the slot** and let the DecisionRow text carry it.

### c. Beacon is a build job, not a capture job

No access, a couple of Figma files, mostly memory. Every Beacon slot is a
recreation, and each one needs a caption that says so. "Recreation" or
"reconstruction," not "dummy data." Dummy data implies you are showing the real
interface with the numbers swapped out. You are not.

Send me the Figma files. What is in them determines whether the Beacon slots are
faithful reconstructions or illustrations.

## 5. Two rules for every slot

**Caption honestly.** Capture, recreation, reconstruction, diagram. A reader who
discovers that a "screenshot" was a recreation stops trusting the writing too,
and the writing is the strongest thing you have.

**No artifact invents a number.** The outcome slots are where this gets tested.
If a chart needs data you do not have, the slot goes away. An empty frame costs
you less than one fabricated figure.
